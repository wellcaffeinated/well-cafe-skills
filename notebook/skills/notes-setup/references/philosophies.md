# Organizing philosophies

Starting lenses, not boxes. Offer a few so the user can *recognize* what fits — a newcomer often doesn't know the options exist. Whatever they pick, structure should still emerge from their use-cases (see [interview](interview.md)); a philosophy just gives a vocabulary and a default shape to start from. Mixing is normal and common — PARA folders with Zettelkasten-style linking, for instance.

| Philosophy | Optimizes for | Shape | Fits when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PARA** | actionability / lifecycle status | Projects · Areas · Resources · Archive | lots of active work; "where is this in its lifecycle?" |
| **Zettelkasten** (atomic + linked) | emergent connection | many small notes, few folders, dense `[[links]]` | thinking and writing; ideas meant to compound over time |
| **Johnny.Decimal** | findability by address | numbered `area.category` system, one home per thing | wants a place for everything and dislikes ambiguity |
| **MOC / navigable hubs** | hand-built navigation | index notes that link outward; folders optional | a large vault navigated by curated hubs, not the tree |
| **Minimal (flat + tags/search)** | low friction | one or a few folders; lean on search and tags | small or new vault; capture-first, let structure grow |

Framing to give the user: *folders carry your one dominant axis cheaply; links carry connection; tags carry topic. Most "philosophies" are just different bets on which of those to lead with* — so the choice is really "what do I most want to be effortless?" See [primitives](primitives.md).

Don't ask the user to commit hard. Pick a lens to start, build from their actual use-cases, and let it drift toward what they use.
